## Executive Summary

Clerky is an AI-powered legal practice management platform purpose-built for attorneys practicing in Kansas and Missouri. Developed by Elev8 AI Solutions & Services, Clerky combines four specialized AI agents (powered by CrewAI), deep integrations with CourtListener and Harvard Caselaw APIs, and a modern tech stack (Next.js, Cloudflare Pages, FastAPI) to deliver a platform that no national competitor can match for KS/MO practitioners.

The legal technology market is projected at $30-35 billion in 2026, growing at 8-12% CAGR, yet the solo and small firm segment remains underserved by tools that understand state-specific rules, local court procedures, and regional filing requirements. Clerky addresses this gap directly.

This 18-month marketing plan targets 500 paying subscribers by Month 18, generating $360,000+ in annual recurring revenue (ARR). The strategy focuses on hyper-local credibility, bar association partnerships, content-driven lead generation, and a freemium model that lowers the barrier to adoption. Total marketing investment over 18 months is budgeted at $126,000, yielding a projected customer acquisition cost (CAC) of $252 per paying user.

The platform is production-ready at v5.2.0 with 81/81 tests passing, 26+ database tables, and 65+ API endpoints, live at lawyrs.pages.dev. This plan moves Clerky from technical readiness to market traction.

## 1. Niche Analysis

### Market Landscape

- The global legal tech market is valued at approximately $30-35 billion in 2025-2026, with compound annual growth rates projected between 8% and 12% through 2030.
- The practice management software sub-segment accounts for roughly $3-4 billion of that total and is growing faster than the overall market due to accelerating cloud adoption among small firms.
- Kansas has approximately 9,200 active attorneys; Missouri has approximately 28,500 active attorneys. Combined, this represents a total addressable market of roughly 37,700 attorneys.
- Solo practitioners and firms with 2-10 attorneys make up roughly 70-75% of all law firms in both states, representing approximately 26,000-28,000 potential users.
- AI adoption in legal practice management is still early. Fewer than 20% of solo and small firm attorneys currently use AI-assisted tools, creating a significant first-mover advantage for specialized solutions.
- Post-pandemic, cloud-based legal tools saw a 40%+ increase in adoption among small firms, and this trend has continued.

**Key takeaway:** No competitor offers a free tier, state-specific AI agents, or integrated access to CourtListener and Harvard Caselaw APIs. Clerky is uniquely positioned to own the KS/MO small firm market.

### Defensible Differentiators

1. **Kansas/Missouri Specialization** - Clerky is built from the ground up for KS/MO practice, including state-specific court rules, filing deadlines, local court procedures, and jurisdiction-aware workflows. National tools treat all 50 states the same.

2. **Four Specialized AI Agents (CrewAI)** - Clerky deploys four distinct AI agents for legal research, document drafting, case analysis, and workflow automation. Competitors offer, at best, a single generic AI assistant.

3. **CourtListener and Harvard Caselaw API Integration** - Direct integration with CourtListener provides real-time access to court opinions, oral arguments, and PACER data. Harvard Caselaw Access Project integration delivers historical caselaw search across 6.7 million cases. No competitor offers both.

4. **Generous Free Tier** - Clerky offers a fully functional free tier, eliminating the financial risk that prevents solo attorneys from trying new software. Every major competitor requires paid commitment from day one.

5. **Modern, Scalable Architecture** - Built on Next.js, Cloudflare Pages, and FastAPI with 65+ API endpoints and 26+ database tables. This architecture delivers sub-second response times globally and scales without infrastructure bottlenecks.

6. **Purpose-Built by a Tech Services Company** - Developed by Elev8 AI Solutions & Services, which brings cross-industry AI expertise to legal tech rather than legacy legal software thinking.

7. **Open Data Philosophy** - By leveraging open legal data sources (CourtListener, Harvard Caselaw), Clerky avoids vendor lock-in on legal data and can pass cost savings to users.

## 2. Marketing Plan

### SMART Objectives

1. **User Acquisition** - Acquire 1,500 registered free-tier users within 18 months, measured monthly via platform analytics.

2. **Paid Conversion** - Convert 33% of free-tier users to paid plans (500 paying subscribers) by Month 18, tracked via billing system.

3. **Revenue Target** - Achieve $30,000 in monthly recurring revenue (MRR) by Month 18, equating to $360,000 ARR.

4. **Bar Association Partnerships** - Secure formal CLE sponsorship or partnership agreements with at least 3 Kansas/Missouri bar associations or local bar groups by Month 9.

5. **Content Authority** - Publish 65+ pieces of KS/MO-specific legal tech content (blog posts, guides, webinars) within 18 months, achieving 5,000+ monthly organic website visitors by Month 12.

6. **Net Promoter Score** - Achieve and maintain an NPS of 50+ among active users by Month 12, measured via quarterly in-app surveys.

7. **Churn Rate** - Reduce monthly churn for paid subscribers to below 5% by Month 12 and below 4% by Month 18.

### Target Personas

**Persona 1: "Solo Attorney Sam"**
- **Demographics:** 35-55 years old, solo practitioner in Kansas or Missouri, 5-15 years of experience
- **Practice Areas:** General practice, family law, criminal defense, estate planning
- **Pain Points:** Cannot afford $100+/month practice management tools; spends 8-10 hours per week on manual legal research; struggles to keep up with KS/MO rule changes; no staff to delegate administrative work to
- **Technology Comfort:** Moderate; uses basic cloud tools (Google Workspace, email) but has not adopted AI; skeptical of "big tech" legal tools
- **Decision Drivers:** Price sensitivity is high; wants to see ROI within 30 days; values tools that understand Kansas/Missouri courts specifically; free trial or free tier is essential
- **Where Sam Gets Information:** Kansas Bar Association and Missouri Bar newsletters, local CLE events, peer recommendations, LinkedIn, legal Facebook groups

**Persona 2: "Small Firm Partner Patricia"**
- **Demographics:** 40-60 years old, managing partner at a 3-8 attorney firm in the Kansas City metro, Wichita, or St. Louis area
- **Practice Areas:** Civil litigation, business law, real estate, personal injury
- **Pain Points:** Currently paying $300-800/month for a practice management tool that does not understand KS/MO courts; associates waste time on research that AI could accelerate; firm needs competitive advantage against larger firms; wants one platform instead of five disconnected tools
- **Technology Comfort:** High; already uses cloud practice management, e-filing systems, and legal research databases; willing to adopt AI if it demonstrates measurable time savings
- **Decision Drivers:** Firm-wide ROI calculation (time saved x hourly rate); data security and compliance; team collaboration features; onboarding speed for associates and paralegals
- **Where Patricia Gets Information:** ABA TECHSHOW, state bar technology committees, legal tech podcasts (Lawyerist, Legal Talk Network), peer referrals from other managing partners

**Persona 3: "Paralegal Paula"**
- **Demographics:** 28-45 years old, paralegal or legal assistant at a small-to-mid firm in Kansas or Missouri
- **Practice Areas:** Supports attorneys across multiple practice areas
- **Pain Points:** Spends significant time on repetitive document preparation, court deadline tracking, and case research; often the person who actually evaluates and recommends new software to attorneys; frustrated by clunky legacy tools that were not designed for paralegal workflows
- **Technology Comfort:** High; often the most tech-savvy person in the firm; comfortable learning new software quickly; active in paralegal association communities
- **Decision Drivers:** Ease of use; time savings on daily tasks; ability to demonstrate value to supervising attorneys; features that make her job easier, not harder
- **Where Paula Gets Information:** Kansas Paralegal Association, Missouri Paralegal Association, paralegal Facebook groups, webinars, peer recommendations

## 5. Cross-Reference Analysis

### Critical Feature Gaps

1. **Mobile Application** - Attorneys need mobile access for court appearances and on-the-go case management. A native iOS/Android app or robust progressive web app (PWA) is essential by Month 9. Without this, Clerky will lose to competitors who offer mobile apps.

2. **E-Filing Integration** - Direct integration with Kansas and Missouri e-filing systems (Kansas eFlex, Missouri eCourts) would significantly reduce manual work. This is a high-value differentiator that competitors struggle with at the state level.

3. **Billing and Invoicing** - While Clerky focuses on practice management and AI research, the lack of integrated time tracking, billing, and invoicing will force users to maintain a second tool. This is a top-3 feature request for any practice management platform.

4. **Client Communication Portal** - A secure client-facing portal for document sharing, messaging, and case updates is expected in modern practice management. The Pro tier includes a basic client portal, but it needs to be feature-competitive.

5. **Trust Accounting / IOLTA Compliance** - Kansas and Missouri have specific rules governing client trust accounts. Built-in trust accounting that complies with KS/MO bar requirements would be a significant selling point and competitive moat.

6. **Offline Access** - Rural Kansas and Missouri attorneys may have unreliable internet. Basic offline functionality (viewing case data, drafting documents) with sync-when-connected capability would address an underserved need.

### Top Opportunities

1. **KS/MO Bar Association "Preferred Vendor" Status** - Achieving endorsed or preferred vendor status with the Kansas Bar Association or Missouri Bar would provide unmatched credibility and access to the full membership for marketing. This single partnership could be worth more than the entire paid advertising budget.

2. **CLE Provider Certification** - Becoming an accredited CLE provider allows Clerky to host educational events that attorneys need for license renewal, creating a recurring engagement channel that competitors cannot easily replicate.

3. **Legal Aid and Pro Bono Integration** - Partnering with Kansas Legal Services, Legal Aid of Western Missouri, or similar organizations to offer free Clerky access for pro bono work builds goodwill, generates press coverage, and introduces attorneys to the platform.

4. **Law School Pipeline** - Offering free Clerky access to law students at KU, MU, Washburn, and UMKC creates brand familiarity before attorneys enter practice. Students who learn on Clerky will advocate for it at their first firms.

5. **Insurance Carrier Partnerships** - Legal malpractice insurers in KS/MO could offer premium discounts to Clerky users based on reduced risk from automated deadline tracking and compliance features. This creates a financial incentive for adoption beyond the software itself.

6. **Rural Attorney Focus** - Rural Kansas and Missouri counties are severely underserved by legal tech. A targeted campaign for rural practitioners, emphasizing AI assistance that compensates for lack of staff, could capture a loyal segment that national competitors ignore entirely.

### Key Risks

1. **Attorney Resistance to AI** - Many attorneys remain skeptical of AI in legal practice due to concerns about accuracy, ethics, and malpractice liability. Mitigation: Position AI as "attorney-supervised assistance," emphasize that all AI outputs require attorney review, publish accuracy metrics, and address ethics rules directly in marketing materials.

2. **Competitor Response** - Clio, MyCase, or another well-funded competitor could launch KS/MO-specific features or acquire a regional player. Mitigation: Build deep local relationships (bar associations, law schools, legal aid) that create switching costs beyond software features. Move fast to establish brand association between "KS/MO legal tech" and "Clerky."

3. **Bar Association Regulatory Concerns** - Bar associations or ethics committees could raise concerns about AI-generated legal work product. Mitigation: Proactively engage with KS/MO ethics committees, publish Clerky's approach to AI ethics in legal practice, ensure all marketing clearly states that Clerky assists attorneys rather than replacing legal judgment.

4. **Customer Concentration Risk** - If a small number of Firm plan accounts represent a large percentage of revenue, losing one could significantly impact MRR. Mitigation: Maintain a healthy balance between Pro (individual) and Firm (team) subscriptions; target a mix where no single account exceeds 5% of total revenue.

5. **Technical Reliability** - Downtime or data loss would be catastrophic for attorney trust. Mitigation: Cloudflare Pages provides edge-level reliability; implement comprehensive backup strategy; publish uptime SLA (target 99.9%); establish incident communication protocol.

6. **Free Tier Abuse / Insufficient Conversion** - If the free tier is too generous, users may never upgrade. If too restrictive, users will not experience enough value to justify payment. Mitigation: Monitor free-tier usage patterns closely in Phase 1; adjust limits based on data; ensure the upgrade path feels natural rather than punitive.

7. **Market Size Limitations** - The KS/MO market is inherently limited to approximately 37,700 attorneys. At some point, growth requires geographic expansion or moving upmarket. Mitigation: Plan Phase 3 expansion research into adjacent states; consider mid-size firm features for upmarket movement; the 18-month plan focuses on capturing a defensible base before expansion.

8. **Funding and Cash Flow** - At $126,000 in marketing spend over 18 months, Clerky needs to reach cash flow sustainability or secure additional funding. The conservative scenario ($198K ARR) covers marketing costs; the base scenario ($360K ARR) provides meaningful margin. Mitigation: Monitor unit economics monthly; be prepared to reduce marketing spend if conversion rates underperform; prioritize channels with lowest CAC.
